Interactive Design Reference

🎡 12-Hue Color Wheel & Vision Science Guide

Click any slice on the 12-Hue Wheel:
Red (0° • Primary)
Opposite Complement: Green (180°) • Analogous Neighbors: Magenta & Vermilion
1. Primary, Secondary & Tertiary Pigments
• Primary (3): Red, Yellow, Blue — pure base pigments that cannot be mixed from others.
• Secondary (3): Orange (R+Y), Green (Y+B), Purple (B+R) — mixed from two Primaries.
• Tertiary (6): Mixed from a Primary + adjacent Secondary: Vermilion (R+O), Amber Gold (Y+O), Chartreuse (Y+G), Teal (B+G), Indigo (B+P), and Magenta (R+P).
2. Hue, Saturation, Value (Tints, Tones & Shades)
TINT = Hue + White
Raises Value (Lighter)
TONE = Hue + Grey
Softens Saturation
SHADE = Hue + Black
Lowers Value (Darker)
3. Color Wheel Harmonies & 60-30-10 Rule
• Complementary (180°): Directly opposite on the wheel (e.g., Blue ↔ Orange, Red ↔ Green). High contrast!
• Analogous (Adjacent): Side-by-side neighbors on the wheel (e.g., Yellow, Chartreuse, Green).
• Triadic (120° Triangle): 3 equally spaced hues (e.g., Orange, Green, Purple).
• 60-30-10 Rule: 60% calm Dominant base, 30% Secondary structure, 10% vivid Focal Accent.
4. Human Color-Blindness & Animal Vision
• Protanopia / Deuteranopia: Reduced Red or Green cone sensitivity; high-luminance Blues and Golden-Yellows remain clear.
• Tritanopia: Blue-Yellow sensitivity loss; rich Magenta/Crimson stands out clearly.
• Animal Eyes: Dogs see Blue & Yellow; Honeybees see Ultraviolet (UV) purples; Pit Vipers sense warm Infrared heat!
